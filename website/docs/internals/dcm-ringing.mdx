# DCM Coil Ringing

In discontinuous conduction mode (DCM), the LS rectifier turns off when the inductor current `i_L` reaches zero. At
that moment the coil stores no energy, but the switch node sits at 0 V while its equilibrium is `V_out`. The node
capacitance (`C_oss` of both FETs plus the PCB) and the coil `L` form an LC tank, which rings around `V_out`.

This page covers what happens *after* the LS FET turns off at the zero crossing. The companion page
[Diode Emulation](diode-emulation.md) covers the zero-crossing detection (ZCD) timing.

## DCM ring and HF transition ring

The switch node shows two distinct ringing phenomena:

- **HF transition ringing** occurs at *every* HS/LS switching edge. The parasitic loop inductance (trace and
  package, ~10–30 nH) resonates with `C_oss` at tens to hundreds of MHz. The ring lasts a few ns, and snubbers,
  gate resistance and layout address it. Most app notes (TI slyt465, Specter Engineering, ADI) focus on this ring.
- **DCM coil ringing** (this page) occurs *only after* the inductor current reaches zero and both FETs are off.
  The main power inductor `L` resonates with the switch-node capacitance `C_sw` (see [LC tank](#lc-tank)), well
  above `f_sw` (39 kHz) but far below the HF transition ring. The ring lasts hundreds of ns to µs.

Because the DCM ring is much slower, firmware can time events against it: `T_ring/4` spans tens to hundreds of
PWM ticks (one MCPWM tick is 6.25 ns).

## LC tank

<SwitchNodeTank />

The schematic shows the power stage during the ring. Both gates are held off, so each FET reduces to its body diode
and its output capacitance `C_oss` (`C_ds + C_gd`). The HS body diode clamps the node one diode drop above `V_in`.

`C_in` and `C_out` are orders of magnitude larger than `C_sw`, so at the ring frequency they act as shorts: `V_in`
and `V_out` stay still while the node swings. The HS `C_oss` therefore returns to ground through `C_in`, and the HS
and LS `C_oss` and the PCB capacitance all sit in parallel from the switch node to ground.

Reduced to the parts that ring, the circuit is a parallel LC tank:

<SwitchNodeTankSimple />

`C_sw` is the sum of the capacitances on the switch node, with `n_HS` and `n_LS` the number of paralleled FETs at
each position:

$$
C_{sw} = n_{HS} \cdot C_{oss,HS} + n_{LS} \cdot C_{oss,LS} + C_{pcb}
$$

`C_oss` depends on the drain-source voltage. With the node near 0 V, the HS FET sees `V_DS ≈ V_in` and the LS FET
`V_DS ≈ 0`. Over the swing, the two move in opposite directions. `C_sw` is therefore an effective value over the
swing, which is what a fit to the ring measures (see
[Switch-node capacitance](../lab/switch-node-capacitance.md)).

The coil `L` and `C_sw` set the ring frequency and the quarter period:

$$
f_r = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot \sqrt{L \cdot C_{sw}}}, \qquad \frac{T_{ring}}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2} \cdot \sqrt{L \cdot C_{sw}}
$$

Losses damp the ring. With `R` the series loss resistance of the tank, its quality factor is

$$
Q = \frac{1}{R} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{L}{C_{sw}}}
$$

and the observed (damped) frequency is

$$
f_d = f_r \cdot \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{4Q^2}}
$$

which is within 1 % of `f_r` for `Q ≥ 4`.

## Ring frequency per board

`C_sw` differs per board and depends mostly on how many FETs are paralleled, so it isn't a universal constant. An early
estimate of ~100–200 pF, which gives 1.3–2.3 MHz, is roughly an order of magnitude low for boards with paralleled
100 V FETs.

The following table lists four boards and that early estimate. Boards A and B are field converters, boards C and D
bench converters; the letters match the [Coil inductance](../lab/coil-inductance.md) and
[Switch-node capacitance](../lab/switch-node-capacitance.md) pages.

| Board | `L` | `C_sw` | `f_r` | `T_ring/4` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| A (field) | ≈51 µH (measured) | ≈4.7 nF (inferred) | 324–330 kHz | ~765 ns |
| B (field) | ≈80 µH (measured) | ≈4.9 nF (inferred) | 248–257 kHz | ~990 ns |
| C (bench) | 83 µH (measured) | 2.98–3.04 nF (measured) | 320 kHz | ~780 ns |
| D (bench) | 40 µH (`coil.conf::L0`) | unmeasured | — | — |
| early estimate | 50–80 µH | 100–200 pF (assumed) | 1.3–2.3 MHz | 111–199 ns |

The ring frequency of boards A and B comes from the periodic `Iout` ripple of DCM duty sweeps, without a scope, and
`C_sw` follows from it and the measured `L` (see
[Switch-node ringing in DCM](../lab/coil-inductance.md#switch-node-ringing-in-dcm)). Board C carries a single
IPP022N12NM6 on the low side. Board D carries 2× IPP050N10NF2S (HS) and 2× IPP039N10N5 (LS), which puts four 100 V
dies on the node. `C_oss` rises steeply toward 0 V, which is where this ring lives.

[Switch-node capacitance](../lab/switch-node-capacitance.md) describes how the board C value was measured, why
board D's existing captures do not give one, and the uncertainty of the resonant model behind the fit.

## Reduction techniques

This section covers hardware techniques: they change components or the board layout. Only an RC
snubber damps the DCM coil ring; gate resistance, PCB layout and a parallel Schottky act on the
[HF transition ring](#dcm-ring-and-hf-transition-ring). The
[firmware techniques](#firmware-techniques) need no board change.

### RC snubber

An RC snubber is a series R+C from the switch node to ground, placed as close to the LS FET as
possible. For the DCM ring, the tank is the main coil `L` and `C_sw`, both known (see
[Ring frequency per board](#ring-frequency-per-board)), so no parasitic needs to be extracted:

- `R` near the tank's characteristic impedance `√(L / C_sw)` damps it critically. Board C
  (83 µH, ~3 nF) gives about 166 Ω.
- `C_snub` must be several times `C_sw`, so that its impedance at `f_r` is small against `R`.
  At 3–10 × `C_sw` that is about 9–30 nF on board C.
- The snubber capacitor is charged and discharged once per switching cycle, so its loss is about
  `C_snub · V_in² · f_sw`. On board C (71 V, 39 kHz) that is about 1.8–5.9 W, a large
  loss for damping a ring that is mainly an EMI concern.

The DCM ring does not exceed the switching voltage: it starts at 0 V with an amplitude of at most
`V_out`, and the HS body diode clamps it at `V_in + V_f`. In a nonsynchronous and a synchronous buck alike, it is
therefore primarily an EMI concern, not a reliability one.

### HF transition ring

These techniques act on the ring at every switching edge, whose tank is the commutation-loop
inductance (~10–30 nH) and `C_oss`. They do not change the DCM coil ring, whose tank is the main
coil.

#### Snubber for the HF ring

The standard snubber design procedure (ADI, TI, Severns) targets this ring. It has these steps:

1. Measure the ringing frequency `f_r` on the switch node with a scope (bandwidth limiting off,
   short ground spring).
2. Find the parasitic capacitance. Add capacitance `C_ext` from the switch node to ground until `f_r` drops to
   half. Then `C_par = C_ext / 3` (total capacitance quadrupled halves `f_r`, since `f_r ∝ 1/√(L·C)`).
3. Find the parasitic inductance: `L_par = 1 / (4π² · f_r² · C_par)`.
4. Set `R_snub = √(L_par / C_par)`, the characteristic impedance of the tank. This value critically damps it.
5. Set `C_snub` to 3–10 × `C_par` (ADI says 1–4 ×, TI says 3–10 ×). A larger `C_snub` gives more damping but
   more loss.
6. Estimate the snubber loss, `P = C_snub · V² · f_sw`: the capacitor is charged and discharged once per cycle, and
   each dissipates `½ · C_snub · V²` in `R_snub`.

An EE StackExchange thread on an LM2576 buck describes practical tuning. The calculations give
an approximate value, and the final value should be found empirically: solder in a pot and tune
R for the best waveform. The RC time constant should be at least 3 × the ringing rise time. Don't aim for
critical damping, because a "tidy waveform" costs more efficiency than it's worth. The final
values for that example were 1 nF + 470 Ω.

#### Gate resistance

A higher LS FET turn-off gate resistance slows `dv/dt` at the moment the FET opens. This reduces
the energy injected into the loop-inductance tank, at the cost of higher switching loss. Specter
Engineering showed a near-linear decrease in overshoot vs. gate resistance from 1–20 Ω.

In this firmware the MCPWM dead-time pair and gate driver strength already influence this.
Adjusting the dead-time can marginally affect ringing amplitude.

#### PCB layout

A smaller high-`di/dt` commutation loop lowers overshoot, so keep the input capacitor, HS FET, and
LS FET as physically close as possible. Trace adds ~10 nH per 25 mm. A smaller loop means less
parasitic inductance, less stored energy, and lower overshoot. This is the most effective hardware change
but requires a board respin. Planar/laminated busbars or adjacent PCB power layers add magnetic
field cancellation.

#### Parallel Schottky

A Schottky diode in parallel with the LS FET body diode has near-zero reverse recovery current,
which reduces the current step that excites the HF ring at turn-off. Specter Engineering's
simulation showed ~90 V overshoot reduction.

## Firmware techniques

### Forced CCM

In continuous conduction mode the inductor current never reaches zero, so DCM ringing never
occurs. The converter can stay in CCM in several ways:

- **Minimum preload** (bleeder resistor on the output): the converter always draws enough
  current to stay in CCM. This is simple but wastes power continuously.
- **Force CCM in firmware**: keep the LS FET on even at zero current, allowing negative
  inductor current (forced PWM). This eliminates DCM ringing entirely but introduces
  circulating current losses, which at light loads can be worse than the ringing.

  This firmware provides it through `converter.conf::forced_pwm=1`, or `sync forced` on the
  console (manual PWM only, `dc N` first). With the default `fpwm_gate=1` this only *requests*
  forced PWM, which takes effect once the duty reaches the voltage ratio (see
  [`fpwm_gate`](../reference/config/converter.md#fpwm_gate-fpwm_gate_margin)). Once effective
  (`forcedPwm_()` in `src/buck.h`), `computeDCM` returns false and the LS runs complementary.
  At a light-load DCM point the duty is below the voltage ratio, so the gated
  `sync forced`/`forced_pwm=1` does not engage there by itself.

  `sync forced!` (or `fpwm_gate=0`) engages immediately. That is **only safe on an output that
  cannot sink**, because forced PWM lets the inductor current go negative.
- **Higher inductance** (hardware): lowers the critical load boundary
  (`I_crit = ΔI_L / 2 ∝ 1/L`), extending CCM to lower loads. The trade-offs are a larger coil,
  more copper loss, less saturation headroom, and slower current slew. Lower inductance does the
  opposite.

### Pulse skipping and burst mode

At light loads, the converter can skip switching pulses entirely instead of operating in DCM. It
fires a burst of CCM pulses to charge the output, then goes idle until `V_out` droops below a
threshold. Each pulse is a CCM pulse, so DCM ringing doesn't occur. The trade-offs are
lower-frequency output ripple at the burst frequency and potential audible noise. Many IC
controllers (onsemi FAN65008, TI LM5146) do this automatically.

### Active damping

Active damping would switch a FET during the ring so that the FET absorbs the ring energy. In the ideal
`L`–`C_sw` model no switching pattern can do this. From 0 V at LS turn-off, the free ring follows

$$
v(t) = V_{out} \cdot (1 - \cos(\omega_r \cdot t)), \qquad \omega_r = 2\pi \cdot f_r
$$

It circles around `V_out` with an amplitude of `V_out`: the node swings between 0 V and `2·V_out`, with minima at
`t = 0` and `t = T_ring` and the coil current crossing zero at both extremes. At `T_ring/4` the node crosses `V_out`
and the reverse coil current is at its peak.

A FET can only pin the node to 0 V (LS) or to `V_in` (HS). Both are at least `V_out` (or `V_in − V_out`) away from
the centre of the ring, so after any pulse the ring has at least the amplitude `V_out` it started with.

<ActiveDampingChart />

The chart compares the free ring with a 200 ns LS pulse at two times:

- **At `T_ring/4` after LS turn-off**, the node is at `V_out` and the reverse coil current is at its peak. The LS
  FET discharges `C_sw` hard to 0 V, and while it conducts the coil current ramps further negative at `V_out/L`,
  drawing energy from the output.

  The ring grows: in the lossless model from 27 V to about 39–51 V for pulses of 20–300 ns. When the larger swing
  reaches `V_in`, the HS body diode clamps it and returns part of that energy to the input. The loss in
  `R_ds(on)` during the pulse is small and does not remove the ring energy.
- **At the first valley, one full ring period after LS turn-off**, the node is near 0 V with near-zero current. In
  the lossless model the pulse switches without loss and the ring keeps its amplitude; a longer pulse enlarges it
  (27 V to 31.5 V for 300 ns).

  With damping, the valley sits a few volts above 0 V, so the pulse discharges `C_sw` hard and the ring grows (in
  the chart, from 19.7 V to 29 V). A brief LS re-trigger at a current zero crossing, the "active clamp" of
  US8933635B2 for LED drivers, is this case.

The same applies to `rect_offset_ns` (`coil.conf`, calibrated per board; `rectOnOffset` in `src/buck.h`), which
delays LS turn-off past the zero crossing so that a small reverse current flows. At turn-off that current starts
the ring with a larger amplitude, `√(V_out² + (Z_0 · i)²)` with `Z_0 = √(L / C_sw)`, instead of damping it.
Increasing it also pushes the operating point toward the reverse-current cliff (see
[Diode Emulation](diode-emulation.md)).

What switch timing can do is choose *when* the next HS edge happens. Turning HS on at a ring valley (valley
switching) lowers the `C_sw · V²` turn-on loss; it does not damp the ring.

The valley time is per board: the first valley is one ring period after LS turn-off, about 3.1 µs on board C and
0.4–0.8 µs for the early estimate. A timing hard-coded to the estimate's `T_ring/4` (111–199 ns) would act at
4–6 % of board C's period, near the start of its ring, where the node is still close to 0 V. Derive the timing
from the board's own `L0` and FET population, not from a constant (see
[Ring frequency per board](#ring-frequency-per-board)).

## Summary

The following table compares the reduction techniques:

| Technique | Ring | HW/FW | Effectiveness | Efficiency cost | No board change? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RC snubber | DCM and HF | HW | High | High for DCM (`C·V²·f`), low–moderate for HF | No |
| Gate R tuning | HF | HW/FW | Medium | Moderate | Partially (dead-time) |
| Layout optimization | HF | HW | High | None | No (respin) |
| Schottky parallel diode | HF | HW | Medium | Low | No |
| Forced CCM | DCM | FW | Complete | High at light load | Yes |
| Pulse skipping / burst | DCM | FW | High | Low | Yes |
| Active damping (LS pulse) | DCM | FW | None: an LS pulse during the ring keeps or grows it | Low | Yes |

For this project, the most promising approach that needs no hardware change is burst/pulse-skip at light load,
which builds on the existing diode-emulation logic in `src/buck.h`. An LS pulse during the ring, including a short
re-trigger at a valley, does not reduce it in the ideal model; valley switching of the next HS edge lowers the
turn-on loss but does not damp the ring.
